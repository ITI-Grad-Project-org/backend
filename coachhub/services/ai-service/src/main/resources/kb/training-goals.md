# Training for specific goals

## Hypertrophy training

Muscle growth responds primarily to hard sets taken close to failure across a wide range of rep schemes. Anything from about 5 to 30 reps builds muscle when the set is taken within a few reps of failure, so the practical range for most work is 6 to 15 reps. Volume is the main driver once effort is sufficient. Prioritise a controlled lowering phase, a full range of motion, and progressing the load over time rather than chasing novel exercises.

## Strength training

Maximal strength is a skill as much as an adaptation. It responds to heavier loads and lower reps — typically 1 to 6 reps at 80 percent of one-rep max or above — with long rest periods and higher frequency on the specific lift being trained. Technique consistency matters more than variety. Accessory work supports the main lift by strengthening the position where it fails, not by adding unrelated volume.

## Fat loss training

Training during fat loss should look much like training during maintenance: keep the load and the intensity, and if anything reduce total volume rather than intensity. Dropping to light weights and high reps "to tone" is the most common mistake and it costs the client muscle. Resistance training is what preserves lean mass in a deficit; the deficit itself does the fat loss. Cardio is a tool for increasing energy expenditure, not a replacement for lifting.

## Endurance and conditioning

Cardiovascular fitness improves with a mix of low-intensity steady work and shorter high-intensity intervals. A common structure is the majority of weekly cardio minutes at conversational effort, with one or two shorter interval sessions. Place hard conditioning away from heavy lower-body lifting where possible — at least a few hours apart, ideally on separate days — because the interference is largest between high-intensity cardio and leg strength work.

## General health and beginners

For a client whose goal is general health, the floor is roughly 150 minutes of moderate activity per week plus two full-body resistance sessions. That prescription alone produces most of the health benefit available from exercise. For an absolute beginner, the priorities in order are turning up consistently, learning the basic movement patterns with acceptable technique, and only then adding load. Complexity added before consistency is established is usually wasted.

## Estimating one-rep max

The Epley formula estimates a one-rep max as weight multiplied by (1 + reps ÷ 30). It is reasonably accurate up to about 10 to 12 reps and progressively overestimates beyond that, so estimates from high-rep sets should be treated as unreliable. Use estimated maxes to set training percentages and to track strength trends over time, not as a number to be tested frequently — true max testing carries a fatigue and injury cost that rarely pays for itself in general population clients.
